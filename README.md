<section id="history">
  <h2>History of the Coloured People in South Africa</h2>
  <p>
    The term "Coloured" in South Africa refers to a diverse group of people with mixed ancestry, primarily descending from the indigenous Khoi and San, enslaved Africans, Europeans, and Asians. Under apartheid, the government enforced this classification as part of a strategy to divide and rule.
  </p>
  <p>
    Historically marginalized, Coloured people were restricted in access to land, education, and employment. Despite this, they developed rich communities, languages (including Afrikaaps), and cultural practices that remain vibrant today.
  </p>
</section>

<section id="khoi-san">
  <h2>Khoi and San Roots</h2>
  <p>
    The Khoikhoi and San were the original inhabitants of southern Africa. Known collectively as the Khoisan, they lived as herders and hunter-gatherers for thousands of years. Their deep knowledge of the land and unique click languages are part of the cultural DNA of Coloured identity.
  </p>
  <p>
    Colonization and genocide reduced their population drastically, and many Khoisan descendants became part of the modern Coloured population. Reviving and respecting Khoisan heritage is vital to honouring the true history of South Africa.
  </p>
</section>

<section id="present">
  <h2>The Current State of Coloured People</h2>
  <p>
    Today, Coloured communities continue to face socio-economic challenges, including underrepresentation in politics, high unemployment, and limited educational opportunities. In many urban areas like Cape Town, gang violence and poverty are persistent issues.
  </p>
  <p>
    However, there is a strong cultural revival among young Coloured people, reclaiming pride in language, dress, and identity. Movements are growing that demand recognition and redress for historic injustices, while celebrating the resilience and vibrancy of Coloured culture.
  </p>
</section>
